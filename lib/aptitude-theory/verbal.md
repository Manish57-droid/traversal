=== Synonyms
---
A **synonym** is a word with the same, or nearly the same, meaning as another word. In synonym questions you pick the option **closest in meaning** to the given word. It does not have to be a perfect match.

### Strategy

1. **Make your own guess first.** Before reading the options, think of a simple word that means the same. Then look for it, or something close, among the options.
2. **Rule out the antonyms.** Exams often include the opposite word as a trap. Cross it out at once.
3. **Check the part of speech.** The answer must be the same type of word: a noun for a noun, an adjective for an adjective.
4. **Use it in a sentence.** Put each option in place of the word in a simple sentence. The one that keeps the meaning is the answer.

### Shortcut 1 — Roots, prefixes and suffixes

Most hard words are built from parts you can learn once and reuse.

| Part | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| bene- | good | benevolent, benefit, benefactor |
| mal- | bad | malevolent, malice, malfunction |
| omni- | all | omnipresent, omnipotent, omniscient |
| ubiq- | everywhere | ubiquitous |
| chron- | time | chronic, chronological |
| -phile | lover of | bibliophile |
| loqu- / locu- | speak | loquacious, eloquent |
| ver- | truth | verify, veracity |
| ami- / amor- | love, friend | amicable, amorous |
| magn- | great | magnanimous, magnify |
| -cide | killing | homicide, pesticide |

```calc
"Magnanimous" → magn (great) + animus (mind/spirit)
→ great-spirited → generous, forgiving
```

### Shortcut 2 — Positive or negative?

Even if you don't know a word, you can often tell whether it sounds positive or negative. Remove the options with the wrong "feel". For example, *Pernicious* sounds negative, so a positive option like "helpful" can be ruled out.

### High-frequency synonym pairs

| Word | Synonym | Word | Synonym |
|---|---|---|---|
| Abundant | Plentiful | Candid | Frank |
| Benevolent | Kind | Diligent | Hardworking |
| Ephemeral | Fleeting | Meticulous | Careful |
| Pragmatic | Practical | Ubiquitous | Omnipresent |
| Lucid | Clear | Obstinate | Stubborn |
| Prudent | Wise | Frugal | Thrifty |
| Gregarious | Sociable | Tranquil | Calm |
| Elated | Overjoyed | Verbose | Wordy |
| Adversary | Opponent | Eminent | Distinguished |

### Build vocabulary every day
- Learn words in **groups by theme** (for example, words for "talkative": loquacious, verbose, garrulous).
- Learn each new word **with its antonym**. One effort covers both question types.
- Read a newspaper editorial daily and note the unfamiliar words.

### Common traps
- **Look-alike words:** *eminent* (famous) vs *imminent* (about to happen).
- A word can have several meanings. Choose the option that matches the common meaning unless context is given.

=== Antonyms
---
An **antonym** is a word with the **opposite** meaning. Antonym questions test the same vocabulary as synonyms, but with a twist: the answer is the word that means the reverse.

### Strategy

1. **Find the word's meaning first**, then think of its opposite before looking at the options.
2. **Watch for the synonym trap.** Exams almost always put a **synonym** among the options. It is the most tempting wrong answer. Cross it out.
3. **Match the strength.** If the word is strong (*furious*), choose a strongly opposite word (*calm*) rather than a weak one.
4. **Match the part of speech.** The antonym of an adjective must be an adjective.

### Shortcut 1 — Prefixes that make opposites

| Prefix | Example |
|---|---|
| un- | happy → unhappy |
| in- / im- / il- / ir- | possible → impossible, legal → illegal, regular → irregular |
| dis- | honest → dishonest |
| non- | violent → nonviolent |
| mal- / bene- | malevolent ↔ benevolent |
| mis- | fortune → misfortune |

> **Careful:** not every "in-" word is a negative. *Inflammable* means the same as *flammable* (easily set on fire).

### Shortcut 2 — Elimination

If three options are near-synonyms of each other (or of the given word), the odd one out is usually the answer.

```calc
Opposite of "Abundant": Scarce / Plentiful / Rich / Ample
Plentiful, Rich and Ample all mean "a lot" → they are synonyms
The odd one out → Scarce
```

### High-frequency antonym pairs

| Word | Antonym | Word | Antonym |
|---|---|---|---|
| Abundant | Scarce | Generous | Stingy |
| Ancient | Modern | Optimistic | Pessimistic |
| Benevolent | Malevolent | Candid | Deceptive |
| Diligent | Lazy | Meticulous | Careless |
| Brave | Cowardly | Humble | Arrogant |
| Transparent | Opaque | Expand | Contract |
| Novice | Expert | Praise | Criticise |
| Frugal | Extravagant | Temporary | Permanent |
| Hostile | Friendly | Obscure | Clear |

### Common traps
- Choosing the synonym because it is the word you recognise.
- Picking a word that is merely *different*, not *opposite*. The opposite of "hot" is "cold", not "wet".

=== One-word Substitution
---
One-word substitution asks you to replace a phrase or description with a **single word**. For example, "a person who can speak many languages" is a **polyglot**. These words are mostly built from Greek and Latin roots, so learning a few roots unlocks many answers.

### Shortcut 1 — Learn the roots

| Root | Meaning | Words |
|---|---|---|
| -ologist | one who studies | ornithologist (birds), entomologist (insects), geologist (earth) |
| -cracy | rule by | democracy (people), autocracy (one person), plutocracy (the wealthy), theocracy (religion) |
| -cide | killing | homicide (a person), regicide (a king), fratricide (a brother), genocide (a race) |
| -phobia | fear of | claustrophobia (closed spaces), acrophobia (heights), hydrophobia (water) |
| -vore | eater | herbivore (plants), carnivore (meat), omnivore (both) |
| poly- | many | polyglot (languages), polygamy (spouses) |
| mono- | one | monogamy, monologue, monotheism |
| -archy | government | monarchy (a king), anarchy (no government), oligarchy (a few) |

### Shortcut 2 — Ages

| Age | Word |
|---|---|
| 60–69 | Sexagenarian |
| 70–79 | Septuagenarian |
| 80–89 | Octogenarian |
| 90–99 | Nonagenarian |
| 100+ | Centenarian |

```calc
"A person aged between 80 and 90" → octo = eight → Octogenarian
```

### Shortcut 3 — Pairs that are easy to confuse

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Emigrant** | leaves their own country to settle elsewhere (exit) |
| **Immigrant** | comes into a new country to settle (in) |
| **Atheist** | does not believe in God |
| **Agnostic** | believes God's existence cannot be known |
| **Altruist** | puts others' welfare first |
| **Egoist** | thinks mainly of themselves |
| **Optimist** / **Pessimist** | expects the best / expects the worst |

### Common one-word substitutions

| Description | Word |
|---|---|
| Place where coins are made | Mint |
| Word that reads the same backward and forward | Palindrome |
| Works without being paid | Volunteer |
| Cannot be avoided | Inevitable |
| Cannot be corrected | Incorrigible |
| Cannot be defeated | Invincible / Indomitable |
| Can be understood in only one way | Unambiguous |
| Lasts for a very short time | Ephemeral |
| Medicine that counters a poison | Antidote |
| Life story written by the person themselves | Autobiography |
| Person who loves books | Bibliophile |
| List of items to be discussed at a meeting | Agenda |
| Place where dead bodies are kept | Mortuary |
| Handwriting that cannot be read | Illegible |
| Person who walks in their sleep | Somnambulist |

### Strategy
1. Find the **key idea** in the description (studies, rule, fear, killing, age).
2. Match it to the **root**.
3. Eliminate options that are related but not exact. A *linguist* studies languages, but a *polyglot* speaks many.

=== Fill in the Blanks
---
Fill in the blanks questions test **grammar** (verb forms, articles, prepositions) and **vocabulary** (choosing the word that fits the meaning). Read the **whole sentence** first. The clue is often after the blank.

### Grammar rule 1 — Subject–verb agreement

A singular subject takes a singular verb; a plural subject takes a plural verb.

- She **goes** / They **go**.
- **Each, every, everyone, either, neither** take a singular verb: *Each of the students **has** submitted.*
- **Neither…nor / either…or**: the verb agrees with the **nearer** subject: *Neither the manager nor the employees **were** satisfied.*
- **Collective nouns** (committee, team, jury) are singular when they act as one unit: *The committee **is** yet to announce.*
- **"The number of"** takes a singular verb; **"a number of"** takes a plural verb.

### Grammar rule 2 — Tenses

| Signal | Tense | Example |
|---|---|---|
| since + point in time | Present perfect (continuous) | She has been working here **since 2015**. |
| for + period | Present perfect (continuous) | ...**for five years**. |
| yesterday, ago, last year | Simple past | I met him **yesterday**. |
| by the time + past | Past perfect | By the time we arrived, the film **had started**. |
| while / when (ongoing past action) | Past continuous | They **were playing** when it rained. |

### Grammar rule 3 — Fixed structures

| Structure | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| It is high time | + simple past | It is high time we **changed**. |
| No sooner ... than | had + past participle | No sooner **had** the bell rung **than**... |
| Hardly / Scarcely ... when | had + past participle | Hardly **had** I left **when** it rained. |
| Had I known (3rd conditional) | would have + past participle | ...I **would have attended**. |
| If I were you | "were" for all subjects | If he **were** rich... |
| Lest | should + verb | Work hard **lest** you **should** fail. |

### Grammar rule 4 — Articles

- **a** before a consonant **sound**: a university, a one-way street.
- **an** before a vowel **sound**: an hour, an honest man, an MBA.
- **the** for something specific or unique: the sun, the best player.

> Go by the **sound**, not the letter: "an **h**onest" (silent h) but "a **u**nion" (sounds like "you").

### Grammar rule 5 — Prepositions

Some words always take a particular preposition. Learn them as pairs:

| Phrase | Phrase |
|---|---|
| true **to** | senior / junior **to** (not "than") |
| prefer A **to** B | afraid **of** |
| interested **in** | depend **on** |
| agree **with** a person | agree **to** a proposal |
| superior / inferior **to** | married **to** |

### Vocabulary blanks — strategy
1. Find the **clue words** in the sentence: *although, but, however* signal a **contrast**; *and, also, moreover* signal **similarity**; *because, so* signal **cause and effect**.
2. Predict the tone (positive or negative) of the missing word.
3. Try each option in the sentence. Read it aloud in your head.

```calc
"Although he was ___, he never gave up."
"Although" → contrast with "never gave up"
So the blank is something negative: tired / discouraged
```

=== Sentence Correction
---
Sentence correction asks you to spot the grammatically correct sentence, or fix the incorrect part. Most errors come from a **short list of rules**, so check them one by one.

### Checklist — go through it in order

**1. Subject–verb agreement**
- *She **doesn't** like* (not "don't").
- *The number of accidents **has** increased.*
- *Each / Everyone / Neither of ... **is / has***.
- Words between the subject and the verb don't change the verb: *The box of chocolates **is** on the table.*

**2. Verb form after helping verbs**
- After *do / does / did*, use the base verb: *She doesn't **like*** (not "likes").
- After modals (*can, should, must, will*), use the base verb: *He should **go***.

**3. Tense consistency**
- *He **has been going** to school since morning* (an action continuing up to now + "since").
- Do not mix past and present without a reason.

**4. "One of the" + plural noun**
- *He is one of the best **players*** (not "player").

**5. Pronouns**
- *Everyone should do **their** duty* is accepted in modern English.
- Use **between you and me**, not "between you and I" (after a preposition, use the object form).

**6. Comparatives**
- Latin comparatives take **to**, not "than": *senior **to**, junior **to**, superior **to**, inferior **to**, prefer **to***.
- No double comparatives: *more better* is wrong, *better* is right.

**7. Verbs that are not used in continuous forms** (stative verbs)
- *I **agree** with you* (not "am agree" or "am agreeing").
- Also: know, believe, understand, love, own, belong, seem.

**8. Reported speech**
- *He asked **why I was** late* (not "asked that why", and no question word order).

**9. Redundancy** — words that repeat the meaning
- *return back*, *repeat again*, *revert back*, *the reason is because*. Remove the extra word.

**10. Articles and prepositions** — see the rules under Fill in the Blanks.

### Common errors at a glance

| Wrong | Right |
|---|---|
| She don't like coffee. | She doesn't like coffee. |
| One of my friend is a doctor. | One of my friends is a doctor. |
| I am agree with you. | I agree with you. |
| She is senior than me. | She is senior to me. |
| He asked that why I was late. | He asked why I was late. |
| Neither of the answers are correct. | Neither of the answers is correct. |
| I have seen him yesterday. | I saw him yesterday. |
| He is more taller than me. | He is taller than me. |

### Shortcut — Compare the options
The options usually differ in just one or two places. **Look only at the differences** and apply the relevant rule. There's no need to reread every sentence in full.

=== Reading Comprehension
---
Reading comprehension (RC) gives you a passage and asks questions about it. RC rewards **method more than speed**. Answer **only from the passage**, never from what you already know.

### The types of questions

| Type | What it asks | How to approach |
|---|---|---|
| **Main idea** | What the passage is mostly about | Read the first and last sentences; pick the option that covers the whole passage, not one detail |
| **Detail / fact** | A specific piece of information | Scan for the keyword and read that sentence carefully |
| **Inference** | What can be concluded but isn't stated directly | The answer must follow from the passage with no extra assumptions |
| **Vocabulary in context** | What a word means in this passage | Replace the word with each option and check that the sentence still makes sense |
| **Tone / attitude** | How the author feels (critical, neutral, optimistic...) | Look at adjectives and words like *however*, *unfortunately* |

### Strategy

1. **Read the questions first** (quickly), so you know what to look for.
2. **Read the passage once**, actively. Note the main idea of each paragraph in a few words.
3. **Pay attention to signal words:**
   - *However, but, although, yet* → a contrast or change of direction.
   - *Therefore, thus, as a result* → a conclusion.
   - *For example, such as* → supporting detail.
4. **Find proof for each answer.** If you can't point to the line that supports it, the answer is probably wrong.

### Shortcut — Eliminate wrong options

Wrong options usually fall into one of these types:

- **Too extreme:** words like *always, never, all, only, completely*, when the passage is more careful.
- **Out of scope:** true in real life, but not mentioned in the passage.
- **Distorted:** uses words from the passage but changes their meaning.
- **Too narrow:** for a main-idea question, it covers only one detail.

```calc
Passage: "Renewable sources like solar and wind do not deplete resources, but their
availability can be inconsistent, so storage technology is important."
Q: What is a challenge of renewable energy?
✗ "It depletes natural resources"   → contradicts the passage
✗ "It is too expensive"              → out of scope (not mentioned)
✓ "Its availability is inconsistent" → stated directly
```

### Common traps
- Bringing in outside knowledge. If the passage doesn't say it, it isn't the answer.
- Picking an option just because it repeats exact words from the passage. Check that the **meaning** matches.
- Rushing the first read. One careful read is faster than three hurried ones.
